Academic Papers

  • Impact of Margin Cuts on Stock Returns and Trading Value: Evidence from the Vietnamese Stock Market (with Ha Quoc Hung, Vo Pham Anh Khoa and Ngo Phu Thanh)
    Journal of Economics and Finance, 2025  [Abstract | DOI]

    Binding margin constraints can affect asset prices and trading activity, yet their impact in retail-dominated emerging markets remains insufficiently understood. This paper examines stock-level margin eligibility removals (margin cuts) on the Ho Chi Minh Stock Exchange during 2020–2025. Using a matched difference-in-differences design that pairs margin-cut stocks with observationally similar margin-eligible peers, the analysis isolates the effect of security-level funding shocks on prices and trading activity. The results show that margin cut announcements are followed by a sharp and persistent decline in stock prices, with treated stocks underperforming matched controls by approximately five percentage points over the subsequent 30 trading days and exhibiting no evidence of mean reversion. By contrast, trading activity does not contract after margin cuts and instead remains elevated over an extended horizon.

  • World Uncertainty and the Marginal Value of Cash: Suggestive Non-linear Evidence from Six ASEAN Technology Markets (with Ngo Phu Thanh)
    International Journal of Economics and Business Research, accepted, 2026  [Abstract]

    This paper examines how broad-based uncertainty shapes the value that equity investors assign to corporate cash holdings. Using the World Uncertainty Index (WUI) and a panel of listed technology firms across six ASEAN economies over 2000–2024, we embed a quadratic WUI interaction into the Faulkender–Wang excess-return framework. The estimates are consistent with an inverted-U pattern in direction: the marginal value of cash rises with uncertainty at moderate levels and then falls at high uncertainty, with an implied interior turning point within the observed WUI range.

Conference Presentations

  • Market-Relative Temporal Representations for Cross-Sectional Equity Ranking — The 10th Vietnam International Conference in Finance (VICIF 2026) [Abstract]

    Cross-sectional equity selection depends on the relative ordering of securities, yet most ranking systems treat the representation of each security as fixed before ranking begins. This paper develops a framework in which recent market histories are represented according to their relevance within the contemporaneous investment universe. A causal sequence encoder preserves the ordering of each stock's trajectory, a cross-sectional transformation expresses the resulting state relative to other available stocks, and listwise supervision aligns the representation with future relative performance.

  • State-Dependent Graph Portfolio Optimization using Selective State Space Models and Regime-Adaptive Graph Learning — Conference on Optimization, Modeling, Simulation, and Analytics (COMOSA 2026) [Abstract]

    Deep reinforcement learning portfolio systems on emerging markets are constrained by short-context temporal encoders that miss medium-term predictive signals and by correlation-based dependence estimates that lag behind regime shifts. This paper proposes the State-Dependent Graph Portfolio, a framework integrating a selective state space encoder with end-to-end learned, state-conditioned graph construction for reinforcement learning-based allocation. Through input-adaptive gating, the encoder achieves linear-time sequence modeling over 120-day lookback windows, capturing long-range dependencies.

  • Financial Characteristics and Sector Context in Classifying Financial Report Fraud: Evidence from Vietnam — The 2nd Global Conference on Sustainability in Economics, Business and Law [Abstract]

    This study examines where the predictive signal truly resides for classifying financial report fraud in an under-examined emerging market. Using Vietnamese non-financial firms listed on the Ho Chi Minh and Hanoi stock exchanges, we assemble a firm-year panel from 2012 to 2023 with 2,747 observations sourced from Refinitiv. The research objective is to quantify the incremental predictive value of firm-level financial characteristics relative to sector and market context. Tree-based ensembles deliver the strongest discrimination by accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and the area under the ROC curve, with random forest and extreme gradient boosting leading the comparisons.

  • Causality Analysis of Economic Policy Uncertainty and Volatility in Clean Energy Stock Indexes — University of Economics and Law Student Research Award [Abstract]

    This study investigates the causal relationship between economic policy uncertainty (EPU) and the returns of clean energy stock indexes, focusing on the NASDAQ Clean Edge Green Energy Index (CELS), the iShares Global Clean Energy ETF (ICLN), and the MSCI Global Alternative Energy Index (MSCI). Using daily data from January 2015 to January 2025 and applying both Granger causality and VECM methodologies, the analysis explores both short-run and long-run dynamics between U.S. and global EPU indexes and clean energy stock returns. Granger causality results reveal strong short-run unidirectional causality from U.S. EPU to all three clean energy indexes.

  • A Multi-Layer Machine Learning and Reinforcement Learning Framework for Portfolio Optimization in VN-100 — University of Economics and Law Student Research Award [Abstract]

    This study proposes a structured multi-layer machine learning–reinforcement learning framework for portfolio optimization in the VN100 equity market, addressing non-stationarity and market frictions characteristic of emerging markets. The framework integrates gradient boosted decision trees for alpha signal generation, Proximal Policy Optimization for dynamic expert weighting, and rule-based risk constraints for portfolio construction. Using daily equity data from 2018 to 2024, out-of-sample evaluation shows that the framework produces statistically distinguishable cross-sectional rankings, with momentum and mean-reversion signals exhibiting stronger predictive performance than low-volatility and low-beta features.
